# Rating scale

Give each pair two whole numbers. You are not told whether the found item really is the lost one; rate the evidence in front of you.

## Quality, 0 to 10

How good a match is this pair: how much does this found item deserve to be shown to this owner as possibly theirs? Use the whole range.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 10 | Several specific things the owner states are confirmed by the found side, including something distinctive, and nothing differs. |
| 8 | The kind of item and its main features agree and nothing differs, but nothing distinctive confirms it; many items like it exist. |
| 6 | Nothing differs, but the found side tells so little that many items would fit. Or everything fits except one small thing people easily get wrong: a neighbouring shade, a rough count, a guess the owner marks as unsure. |
| 4 | One real difference that a memory slip or careless typing could explain, while the rest agrees. |
| 2 | A clear difference in something the owner would know (colour family, a brand or name, the kind of object, a distinctive part), although the items are the same sort of thing. |
| 0 | Plainly a different item. |

- The finder's photo is the strongest evidence about the found item. Where the finder's words and the photo disagree, the photo is right.
- A different place or date on its own is weak evidence either way: items get moved and people misremember days.
- Reports may be in Arabic, Arabic typed in Latin letters and digits, German, Hindi/Urdu words or English. Read them for what they say.

## Difficulty, 1 to 5

How hard is this pair to rate?

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Obvious at a glance. |
| 3 | Needs careful reading or a close look at the photo. |
| 5 | Careful people would fairly give very different quality scores. |
